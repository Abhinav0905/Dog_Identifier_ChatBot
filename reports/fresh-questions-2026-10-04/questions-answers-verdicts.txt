If you feel **very weak, dizzy, or the bleeding won’t stop**, your parents should get emergency medical help at once.

Q11
Question:
I have ₹5,000 this month to help community dogs in India. How should I prioritise food, vaccination and sterilisation? Please do not invent package prices or promise that sterilisation will immediately stop chasing.

Actual answer:
With ₹5,000, **don’t split it evenly**. Prioritise in this order:

1. **Rabies vaccination first**  
   This gives the biggest public-health benefit for dogs and people. Try to cover the dogs you can identify and reach again, and keep a simple photo/date record.

2. **Sterilisation second**  
   Use the money to help one dog actually get sterilised through a vet/public ABC service if possible—often your money goes furthest on **transport, handling, short recovery support, or part-payment** rather than random feeding. Prioritising **females** usually has the strongest population impact. It may reduce some roaming or mating-related conflict over time, but it will **not reliably or immediately stop chasing**.

3. **Food third, and targeted**  
   Feed only a **small, regular group** you can also monitor for illness, vaccination and sterilisation. Focus on **thin, old, recovering, lactating, or very young** dogs, and include clean water. Avoid expanding feeding to many dogs if you cannot follow through with care.

A practical rule:
- **If vaccination/sterilisation access exists nearby:** put most of the ₹5,000 into **vaccination + getting at least one dog sterilised**, and keep food limited/supportive.  
- **If no procedure access this month:** use the month to **vaccinate reachable dogs** and provide **modest regular feeding/water** to a small monitored group while arranging later sterilisation.

If a dog is **injured, very sick, or hit by a vehicle**, urgent veterinary care comes before routine feeding.

Reason: Distinguishes vaccination, sterilisation and food and avoids invented prices or an immediate chasing cure, but gives a rigid spending order and an "at least one dog" target without checking needs, existing coverage or local costs.
